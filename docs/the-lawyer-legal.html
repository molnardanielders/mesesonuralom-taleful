The lawyer — the legal background

So the programme can launch legally and safely. This is not legal advice — a 'proper' launch needs a lawyer and a data-protection expert.

The role

The law is not an obstacle but the foundation of trust and a clean start. The lawyer's job is to get the legal frameworks right for the licence, the content, children's data and the service — and to make sure the promises made to parents can be kept.

Questions — the first task

Licence: are CC BY-SA 4.0 (concept/documentation) + GPLv3 (code) correct and sufficient, and how should they be credited?

Content: how can the 0–3 story library be obtained legally (rights-holder agreement, territory, term, platform, language)?

Children's data: is a data-protection impact assessment (DPIA) required; what is the minimum GDPR compliance?

Research data: what ethics approval and anonymisation does consent-based data require?

Media law: does it count as a media service (AVMS / national media authority); is there an age-rating obligation?

Subscriber list: on what terms can it be handled (consent, unsubscribe, storage)?

Proper launch: what company form, terms of service and liability frameworks are needed?

International expansion (e.g. SkyShowtime, iQIYI): what differing legal requirements apply?

Key areas

Content and copyright

Licensed or own/CC content; the framework for distribution.

Children's data / GDPR

Consent, DPIA, data minimisation, the parent's rights.

Media law

AVMS / national media authority; age rating, protection of minors.

Consumer protection

Terms of service, transparent pricing, easy cancellation.

Advertising

Strictly regulated for children — the concept is ad-free.

Claims

Avoid medical/therapeutic promises: 'it accompanies, it does not diagnose'.

Guarantees to parents

  • No advertising, no selling data, no ad tracking.

  • Transparency and full parental control over the data (access, deletion, switching off).

  • The system accompanies; it does not diagnose, and it does not replace a professional.

Conditions for a 'proper' launch

  • A legal entity (company), terms of service and a privacy notice.

  • Legally licensed (or own/CC) content.

  • GDPR compliance (DPIA, consent flows) and data security.

  • Any media-law and accessibility compliance in the target country.

  • Involving a lawyer and a data-protection expert.
