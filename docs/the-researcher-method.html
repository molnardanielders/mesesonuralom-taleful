The researcher — the classification method

Story research is the heart of the programme. This document describes how the classification becomes trustworthy enough that a mother or father can believe it with their eyes closed.

The two axes

Every story is scored 0–3 on two separate axes. A story can be high heart and high mind at once — because most good stories develop both, just in different proportions.

Heart

empathy · emotions · connection

  • Emotions are visible and can be named.
  • Someone comforts or helps another.
  • It shows other people's points of view.
  • Friendship, family and belonging at the centre.

Mind

thinking · discovery · problem-solving

  • There is a problem or question to solve.
  • Cause and effect are visible.
  • Discovery: how things work.
  • Concepts: numbers, shapes, words.

The parent's choice (heart or mind) filters on these values — but always within the given age band. Age always comes first.

Further attributes

Length

The episode length in minutes — matched to the age band.

Stickiness

How hard it is to stop (low/medium/high). Before a bath, high is best avoided.

Scary content

None / mild / strong — even at scene or timestamp level.

Developmental-psychology foundations

  • Heart: theory of mind and attachment — perspective-taking typically from ages 3–5; for the youngest, security is the foundation.

  • Mind: concrete, present-bound thinking (Piaget) — visible cause and effect, not abstract tasks.

  • Age and pace: short, slow, repetitive content; under-2s learn poorly from screens.

  • Screen time: the WHO and the AAP urge caution for the youngest — this shapes the age bands and limits.

  • Transitions and routine: a signalled ending and a repeating ritual — the basis of the switch-off experience.

These are textbook-level, established frameworks. Compiling the precise, citable literature and tuning the criteria is the researcher branch's job.

Workflow

Select content (the 0–3 canon first).

Gather metadata and background material.

Independent scoring: every story by at least two experts, independently.

Reconcile differences, measure reliability.

Approve and add to the classification database.

Periodic review.

With parental consent: research data

Human expertise, not machine guesswork.

With the parent's consent, anonymous, aggregated data supports the research — and thus the classification. Never a diagnosis of any individual child.

  • Explicit consent, revocable at any time; off by default.
  • Data minimisation, anonymisation, local processing where possible.
  • Research-ethics approval and GDPR protection (children's data!).
  • The system accompanies; it does not diagnose.
