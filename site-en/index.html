Taleful

The first children’s story channel that knowingly ends.

The parent answers a single question — heart or mind? — and the child closes the story themselves. In an age-appropriate length, with a predictable ending and no struggle. Before bedtime, lunch or heading out — whenever a child needs to stop.

The problem

What every parent of little ones knows

What’s right for them now?

It’s hard to decide quickly which story suits a child at a given age and moment.

The ending is always a battle

Before a bath or bedtime, switching the story off means tears and bargaining.

It never ends

Most children’s content is endless — autoplay always offers just one more.

The solution

A story that has an order to it

Five simple steps that give the decision to the parent and the ending to the child.

Who’s watching now?

The parent selects the child — the system knows their age.

Heart or mind?

The parent answers what they actually know: is it empathy or thinking that’s needed right now?

Age-appropriate story and length

The system offers the right story, in a length that fits the age.

Getting ready and counting down

At the start, the child learns how long it will last; a visible, dwindling indicator counts down.

The child closes it

Why it’s different

Not another screen, but a better habit

A conscious ending, not endless scrolling — the end really is the end.

The parent decides, based on a question they can answer: heart or mind.

The child closes it — the conflict becomes a ritual and a small sense of achievement.

Human expertise, not an algorithm: writers, publishers and researchers classify the stories.

Age-first and development-aware, it supports sensible, watch-together use.

Good for parent and child alike — calmer evenings, fewer battles.

The stars run out — and the child closes the story

The story doesn’t simply stop: it prepares the child for the ending. Storybook stars count down (little ones don’t yet grasp minutes), ‘falling asleep’ one by one, and the same short goodbye comes every time. At the end the child closes the story themselves — and earns a small, calming bit of praise for it. The battle becomes a familiar, safe evening ritual.

The pirate · the content

Building the story library

Finding, selecting, trimming to length and organising the important stories of the 0–3 canon.

  • Four steps: finding → selecting (by age band and heart/mind) → trimming to size (short episodes) → organising.
  • Quality: good picture and sound; one episode = one file; consistent naming.
  • Milestone: the 0–3 story library comes together, in good quality, trimmed to the right length.
  • Boundary: publicly distributing copyrighted content is not part of the open, legal project — the legal route is licensing.

The researcher · the classification

The classification methodology

This is what makes the programme trustworthy — it’s why a parent can believe in it with their eyes closed.

  • Two axes: heart and mind, each scored 0–3; a story can be high on both at once.
  • Attributes: age bands (0–18 months … 4–5 years), length, stickiness, scary content.
  • Reliability: every story is scored by at least two experts, independently of one another.
  • Foundation: grounded in developmental psychology; the classification goes into the scoring template.

The developer · the system

The technical system

A turnkey, open-source solution that anyone can build on.

  • ISO (without content): a lightweight Linux kiosk, a player, the control software, Home Assistant and an empty classification database.
  • Hardware: a mini PC / Raspberry Pi, a TV, a big button (an off-the-shelf wireless one or a DIY arcade button + ESP32).
  • Modules: display, brain (choice + session), classification access, storage, player control, smart home.
  • App and open code: mobile/tablet app; the code under GPLv3.

The lawyer · the legal side

The legal side

So the programme can launch legally and safely. (Not legal advice.)

  • Questions: licensing, content licensing, children’s data / GDPR (DPIA), media law (AVMSD and the national regulator), the mailing list, company structure.
  • Guarantees for parents: no ads, no selling of data, transparency and full parental control.
  • Principle: the system accompanies — it does not diagnose, and it does not replace a professional.

The designer · the look

The visual design

Design decides whether the programme feels friendly, trustworthy and easy to use.

  • Theme system: a clean default (star / heart / mind); the folk-tale Matyó style as an option.
  • Usability-first (0–3): large tap targets, little text, calm animations, an evening mode; nothing that leads away.
  • Designer kit: colours, fonts, motifs — everything needed to carry the dream forward.
  • One visual language: web → documents → control software → app.

Two paths

One way or another — it has to exist

This programme can come to life in two ways. Both paths share the same principles, with open communication and published results.

On our own, at home

A parent builds it for their own children — at home, slowly, from whatever is to hand. It can work for one family, but sourcing the stories lawfully and making them widely, legally available stays unsolved.

Championed

A serious partner — public-service media, for example — licenses the stories and provides a team and resources. That is how it becomes a real, legal product available to every family.

‘I see both paths as viable, and one doesn’t rule out the other — learning from each other is no great effort. For wide distribution, though, a clear conscience, confident teammates and an orderly setting wouldn’t go amiss either.’

— a father

How it can happen

Hungary’s public-service media has everything it takes

A programme like this doesn’t need a vast apparatus. Public-service media already has everything required: significant resources, professional experience and ties to sister institutions — such as research institutes that could provide the developmental-psychology basis for the classification.

Even a small, dedicated team could create a streaming app that benefits children and parents alike and fits the public-service mission.

The concept is already taking shape as a home prototype: the classification framework (heart/mind, age bands, ‘stickiness’), the scoring workflow and the design of the switch-off experience are all ready.
